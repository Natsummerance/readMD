# Growth Execution Kit Spread Review

## Verdict

The kit is suitable for manual distribution planning. It does not predict platform performance; it explains why the selected messages can resonate without turning every channel into the same announcement.

## Five-Theory Decode

1. **Silence spiral:** Many users do not publicly admit that they retype reports, lose citation keys, or rebuild slide decks after every edit. The kit names these handoffs without blaming the reader.
2. **Uses and gratifications:** The primary gratifications are information value and control. Readers learn which boundary applies, what remains local, and where human review is still required.
3. **Framing:** The kit frames ReadMD as a way to keep an existing Markdown source useful, not as a demand to abandon familiar tools.
4. **Two-step flow:** Likely first messengers are technical writers, graduate students, teachers, and support engineers who already answer workflow questions for colleagues.
5. **Cognitive consistency:** The message confirms that people want their notes, citations, and presentations to stay accurate, then challenges the habit of copying content into another tool before every delivery.

## Effective Stance

Stand beside the person who owns a real document under deadline. Promise fewer broken handoffs and clearer limits, not perfect conversion, guaranteed recognition, or automatic growth.

## Channel Resonance Notes

- GitHub rewards responsive issue triage more than announcements.
- X and Mastodon favor one concrete failure mode per post.
- Reddit requires prior participation and a rule check.
- LinkedIn responds to handoff loss in professional documentation.
- Hacker News responds to technical boundaries and source preservation.
- V2EX and Zhihu need Simplified Chinese context rather than translated marketing.
- Xiaohongshu needs a visible before-and-after task with existing captures.
- WeChat works best as a weekly summary, not a daily broadcast.
