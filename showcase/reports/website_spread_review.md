# Website Spread Review

Artifact reviewed: the multilingual ReadMD website answer corpus and launch message: “长文档不再卡住”。

## Five-Dimension Analysis

### 1. Silence Release (Spiral of Silence)

- What the audience withholds: local Markdown users are tired of being told to upload private notes, buy a cloud plan, or rebuild slides before presenting.
- Does the content say it: yes. The site states that core reading, editing, pagination, OCR, and conversion are designed to run locally and do not require a cloud account.
- Theory basis: Neumann’s spiral of silence predicts that a defensible public statement releases a preference users avoid voicing when commercial tools frame cloud workflows as normal.

### 2. Satisfaction Motivation (Uses and Gratifications)

- Activated needs: information value, emotional relief from tool friction, and practical control over one’s files.
- Specific satisfaction: readers learn that an 8,000-line / 500 KB document can be paginated semantically while outline, search, formulas, and navigation remain connected.
- Theory basis: Katz’s framework treats media adoption as active need satisfaction rather than passive exposure.

### 3. Position Frame (Framing Theory)

- Whose side it takes: the person holding the `.md` file, not the platform holding the export button.
- Why it works: “文件留在本地” reframes speed and privacy as ownership and continuity, not as a feature checkbox.
- Theory basis: Entman frames issues through selection and salience; this makes local-first processing the interpretive center instead of an accessory.

### 4. Transmission Entry Point (Two-Step Flow)

- First transmitters: developers who maintain long technical notes, researchers handling citations and formulas, teachers reusing course Markdown, and community members recommending offline tools.
- Motivation: they gain a concrete remedy they can demonstrate in seconds—open a large file, edit it, and present without switching tools.
- Theory basis: Lazarsfeld’s two-step flow emphasizes influence through informed relays who translate utility into social proof.

### 5. Belief Structure (Cognitive Dissonance)

- Existing belief confirmed: Markdown should remain portable and readable across tools.
- Specific behavior challenged: copying the same content into a separate online converter and slide deck after every revision.
- Action impulse: yes. The claim is paired with a direct release path and checksum verification, so trying ReadMD resolves the mismatch rather than merely amplifying dissatisfaction.

## Synthesis

- Underlying emotion: frustration at losing time and custody to fragmented tools.
- Effective stance: stand beside the owner of the original Markdown file; do not position ReadMD as another platform demanding migration.
- What the audience wants next: proof for their exact scenario—long documents, academic references, DOCX/PDF intake, offline OCR, and direct presentation.

## Chatroom Direction

- Core focus: which workflow boundary creates the strongest first-run demonstration: reading a very large document, converting an existing office file, or presenting the same Markdown used for writing?
- Resonance guardrail: keep every claim tied to observable product behavior and repository evidence.
- Avoid: vague “best reader” language, unverifiable performance promises, and framing privacy as fear instead of control.
